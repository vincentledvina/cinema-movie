Licensed amateur radio operators can contribute to MOVIE by hosting a Personal Space Weather Station (PSWS). A PSWS records magnetometer data and high frequency (HF) radio signals, with very low frequency (VLF), GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System), and all-sky imaging versions in development. Stations collect data continuously at a known location, which is what the science needs: measurements that are running whenever a CINEMA satellite passes over.

## Where stations are needed

- **VLF receivers in Canada and Alaska**, ideally near the University of Calgary's all-sky cameras. Only a few VLF receivers operate in that region now, and two or three more would make a real difference to science question 2.
- **HF receivers in the auroral zone.** The amateur HF network is large, but no noise-measuring receiver yet sits inside the auroral zone, between about 60 and 75 degrees geomagnetic latitude. Even a few new stations in Canada and Alaska would add radio paths that cross the aurora directly.
- **Magnetometer and GNSS stations at mid-latitudes**, for the mid-latitude part of science question 1.

## What hosting involves

Hosting means installing the hardware, keeping it powered and connected to the internet, and letting the data flow to the PSWS network. Stations follow HamSCI's existing practices for data sharing and credit.
