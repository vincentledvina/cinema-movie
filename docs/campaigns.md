## Current and upcoming campaigns

There are no campaigns scheduled yet. MOVIE is in its planning phase, and the first campaigns will be announced here and on the email list once it opens.

## How MOVIE campaigns will work

A campaign asks aurora chasers in a target region to photograph the aurora on a specific night and then submit their images to MOVIE's community on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org), where each submission is published with the observer's name and a DOI (digital object identifier) that can be cited.

Campaigns will come in two types:

- **Planned campaigns** are built around a CINEMA satellite pass that is known well in advance. These will be announced a week or more ahead, with the observing window and the science goal stated up front.
- **Short-notice campaigns** respond to geomagnetic activity. Activity from a recurring coronal hole can often be flagged a week to nearly a month ahead. A coronal mass ejection (CME, also called a "solar storm") may give only 48 to 72 hours of notice once its arrival is forecast well enough to name a night.

A short-notice campaign will only be called when a large part of the target region is forecast to be clear and conditions are safe to be out in. No observation is worth an unsafe drive.

## What you will hear, and when

| When | What |
|---|---|
| About a week before | The target region, the type of event, and what to prepare |
| The day before | Go or no-go, timing, expected conditions, and a reminder to check your camera clock |
| The day after | How and where to submit your images |
| About a week after | What was collected, what it showed, and who contributed |
