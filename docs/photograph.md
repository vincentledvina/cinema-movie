Aurora chasers can contribute to MOVIE by photographing the aurora during MOVIE campaigns. Below are the upcoming campaigns, guidance on taking photographs that are useful to the science, and how to submit them.

## Campaigns

### Current and upcoming campaigns

There are no campaigns scheduled yet. MOVIE is in its planning phase, and the first campaigns will be announced here and on the email list once it opens.

### How MOVIE campaigns will work

A campaign asks aurora chasers in a target region to photograph the aurora on a specific night and then submit their images to the [CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie), where each submission is published with the observer's name and a DOI (digital object identifier) that can be cited.

Campaigns will come in two types:

- **Planned campaigns** are built around a CINEMA satellite pass that is known well in advance, and they will be announced a week or more ahead, with the observing window and the science goal stated up front.
- **Short-notice campaigns** respond to geomagnetic activity. Activity from a coronal hole (a region of the Sun where fast solar wind escapes) often returns with the Sun's 27-day rotation, so it can be flagged a week to nearly a month ahead. A cloud of solar plasma (a coronal mass ejection, or CME) may give only 48 to 72 hours of notice once its arrival is forecast well enough to name a night.

A short-notice campaign will only be called when a large part of the target region is forecast to be clear and conditions are safe to be out in. Please do not drive or observe in unsafe conditions for a MOVIE campaign.

## Observing guidance

After a MOVIE campaign night, you can submit your photographs to the CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo, a free research data archive. Each record you submit is published with your name as its creator and gets a DOI (digital object identifier), a permanent link that scientists can cite when they use your images. The steps below are adapted from the procedure HamSCI uses for its Meteor Scatter QSO Party.

Community page: [zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie)

Before you start, read the warning in step 6: a published record cannot be deleted.

### 1. Set your camera clock before the campaign night

Your photographs can only be matched to what the CINEMA satellites saw if MOVIE knows when each one was taken, to within a few seconds.

1. Set the camera clock as close as you can to the correct time. Note whether it is set to local time or UTC (Coordinated Universal Time).
2. At the start of the session, take one photograph of a phone screen showing the current time in UTC, for example from [time.gov](https://time.gov). Keep that photograph with the others and include it in your upload. Anyone can then work out the exact offset of your camera clock from it, even if the clock was off.

### 2. What to write down during the night

- The start and end times of the session, in UTC.
- Where you observed from: the nearest town, and the exact coordinates if you are willing to share them (see step 4).
- The direction the camera was pointing, as a compass direction (for example, north or north-northeast).
- The camera, lens, and exposure settings: shutter speed, aperture, and ISO. For a time-lapse, also note the interval between frames.
- Anything you noticed about the aurora: its form (such as arcs, beads, or streamers), how it moved, its colors, and anything unusual.

### 3. Prepare your files after the night

- **Upload the original image files.** Images saved from social media or messaging apps usually lose the time each frame was taken and the camera settings, which the original files store.
- **Keep the original file names.** The camera's numbering keeps the frames in order.
- **Zip image sequences.** Zenodo accepts at most 100 files per record. For a time-lapse or a long sequence, put the frames in a .zip file. On Windows, select the files, right-click, and choose Send to, then Compressed (zipped) folder. On a Mac, select the files, right-click, and choose Compress.
- **Include the clock photograph** from step 1 as its own file, for example `clock_check.jpg`.
- JPEG files are fine. RAW files are welcome too if you want to share them, though they are much larger.

### 4. Location and privacy

Your record is public, and it cannot be deleted once published, so decide how precise a location to give before you submit.

- If you observed from a public site, such as a park, a pullout, or a dark-sky site, you can give its name and coordinates.
- If you observed from home, give the nearest town in the public record. If you are willing, email your exact coordinates to the MOVIE team at [vincentledvina@gmail.com](mailto:vincentledvina@gmail.com). MOVIE keeps them with the science data and does not publish them.

### 5. Create a Zenodo account

You need a Zenodo account to upload.

1. Go to [zenodo.org](https://zenodo.org) and choose Sign up.
2. You can sign up with ORCID, GitHub, or an email address. We suggest ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID), a free identifier that keeps your contributions linked to you across projects. You can create an ORCID iD at [orcid.org](https://orcid.org).

### 6. Upload your record

1. Sign in to Zenodo, click the + icon at the top right, and choose New upload.
2. Under Community, choose Select a community, search for CINEMA-MOVIE, and select it.
3. Under Files, click Upload files and add your images, .zip files, and the clock photograph.
4. Under "Do you already have a DOI for this upload?", choose No. Zenodo will create a DOI when the record is published.
5. **Resource type:** choose Dataset.
6. **Title:** use this format, with the date in UTC:

   `CINEMA-MOVIE aurora campaign, YYYY-MM-DD UTC, Your Name, Town, State or Province, Country`

   Example: `CINEMA-MOVIE aurora campaign, 2027-03-14 UTC, Jane Smith, Duluth, Minnesota, USA`

   Use the UTC date the session started. You can convert your local time to UTC with a time zone converter, e.g., [worldtimebuddy.com](https://www.worldtimebuddy.com/)
7. **Publication date** fills in automatically. Leave it as it is.
8. **Creators:** add yourself, with the name you want credited. Add your ORCID iD and affiliation if you have them. If someone observed with you, you can add them as a creator too.
9. **Description:** copy the template below and fill it in.
10. **License:** keep the default, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You keep the copyright, and anyone may reuse your images if they credit you.
11. **Keywords:** add `CINEMA-MOVIE`, `aurora`, the campaign date as `campaign YYYY-MM-DD`, and your region (for example, `Upper Midwest`).
12. The sections below Keywords are optional.
13. Click Preview to check the record, then Submit for review. If the record is not ready, click Save draft and come back to it later from your Zenodo dashboard.

**Warning: once a record is published, it cannot be deleted.** Zenodo lets you publish a new version to correct a mistake, but the earlier version stays public.

### Description template

Copy everything below into the Description box, fill in each line, and delete any line that does not apply.

```
Campaign: CINEMA-MOVIE aurora campaign, YYYY-MM-DD
Observer(s):
Session start (UTC):
Session end (UTC):
Location (town, state or province, country):
Coordinates (optional, decimal degrees):
Camera pointing (compass direction and height above the horizon):
Camera make and model:
Lens and focal length:
Exposure settings (shutter speed, aperture, ISO):
Time-lapse interval, if any:
Camera clock set to (UTC or local time zone):
Camera clock offset from UTC, if known (seconds, and how you measured it):
Files included (number of images, .zip files, clock photograph):
What you saw (form, motion, color, anything unusual):
Other notes:
```

Please do not put your email address or phone number in the description. Everything in the record is public.

### 7. What happens next

A MOVIE curator reviews each submission before it is published, usually within a few days of a campaign. The curator checks the title format, the description fields, and the keywords, and may ask you to add something that is missing. Once the record is accepted, it is published with its DOI, and it will be listed with the other campaign records on the [Resources]({{ "/resources/" | relative_url }}) page.

Questions about submitting? Email [vincentledvina@gmail.com](mailto:vincentledvina@gmail.com), or ask in the Aurora Chasers category on the MOVIE forum once it opens.

### Safety

Plan your route before you leave, dress for the cold, and tell someone where you are going. Please do not drive or observe in unsafe conditions for a MOVIE campaign.
