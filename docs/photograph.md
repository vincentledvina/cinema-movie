Aurora chasers can contribute to MOVIE by photographing the aurora during MOVIE campaigns. Below are the upcoming campaigns and guidance on taking photographs that are useful to the science.

## Campaigns

### Current and upcoming campaigns

There are no campaigns scheduled yet. MOVIE is in its planning phase, and the first campaigns will be announced here and on the email list once it opens.

### How MOVIE campaigns will work

A campaign asks aurora chasers in a target region to photograph the aurora on a specific night and then submit their images to the [CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie), where each submission is published with the observer's name and a DOI (digital object identifier) that can be cited.

Campaigns will come in two types:

- **Planned campaigns** are built around a CINEMA satellite pass that is known well in advance, and they will be announced a week or more ahead, with the observing window and the science goal stated up front.
- **Short-notice campaigns** respond to geomagnetic activity. Activity from a coronal hole (a region of the Sun where fast solar wind escapes) often returns with the Sun's 27-day rotation, so it can be flagged a week to nearly a month ahead. A cloud of solar plasma (a coronal mass ejection, or CME) may give only 48 to 72 hours of notice once its arrival is forecast well enough to name a night.

A short-notice campaign will only be called when a large part of the target region is forecast to be clear and conditions are safe to be out in. Please do not drive or observe in unsafe conditions for a MOVIE campaign.

## Observing guidance

Photographs are most useful to the science when MOVIE knows exactly when and where each one was taken and which way the camera was pointing. The sections below cover what to record. The full submission procedure will be posted here before the first campaign.

### Set your camera clock

Camera clocks drift, and many are left on local time or were never set after a battery change. A photo with the wrong time is hard to match to what the satellites saw.

Before a campaign night:

1. Set the camera clock as close as you can to the correct time, and note whether it is set to local time or UTC (Coordinated Universal Time).
2. At the start of the session, take one photo of a phone screen showing the current time in UTC, for example from [time.gov](https://time.gov). The photo of the phone lets anyone work out the exact offset of your camera clock later, even if the clock was off.

### What to record

For each session, write down or keep track of:

- the date and the start and end times, in UTC;
- where you observed from, either exact coordinates or the nearest town;
- the direction the camera was pointing;
- the camera, lens, and exposure settings (shutter speed, aperture, and ISO, the sensor sensitivity);
- anything you noticed about the aurora: its form, motion, or color, and anything unusual.

### Location and privacy

If you observe from home, you can give the nearest town in place of exact coordinates. Published records cannot be deleted, so decide how precise a location to give before you submit.

### Submitting your images

Images will be submitted to the [CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie). Zenodo is a free research data archive hosted by CERN (the European Organization for Nuclear Research). Each record you submit will name you as its creator, carry a DOI (digital object identifier) that can be cited, and be published under a [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) license. You keep the copyright, and anyone may reuse an image if they credit you.

A MOVIE curator will check each submission before it is published. The step-by-step procedure, adapted from the one HamSCI uses for its [Meteor Scatter QSO Party](https://hamsci.org/msqp) (a QSO is a two-way radio contact), will be posted here before the first campaign.

### Safety

Plan your route before you leave, dress for the cold, and tell someone where you are going. Please do not drive or observe in unsafe conditions for a MOVIE campaign.
