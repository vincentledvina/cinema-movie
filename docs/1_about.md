## Mission

MOVIE brings aurora chasers, amateur radio operators, and members of the public into the CINEMA mission as contributors to its science. Volunteers will photograph the aurora during planned campaigns, run ground instruments that record continuously, and label auroral forms in the satellites' movies. MOVIE will announce campaigns with enough notice to plan for, give clear instructions, publish each volunteer's data with their name on it, and report the results back to the volunteers who contributed them.

## CINEMA

CINEMA (Cross-scale INvestigation of Earth's Magnetotail and Aurora) is a NASA Small Explorer mission led by Robyn Millan at Dartmouth College. Its nine small satellites will each carry an auroral imager (AIM), a magnetometer (MAGNET), and a particle spectrometer (PARSEL), to study how Earth's magnetotail drives the aurora and the space weather effects that come with it. The mission is scheduled to launch no earlier than 2030.

## MOVIE

MOVIE (Multi-platform Observations from Volunteers: Ionospheric Experiments) is the mission's citizen science component. Volunteers on the ground will extend what the satellites measure from orbit, in three ways:

| | Aurora chasers | Amateur radio operators | Aurora labelers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What MOVIE asks** | Photographs from a specific place on a specific night during a campaign | Host and maintain a Personal Space Weather Station (PSWS) and keep it collecting data | Label auroral forms (the shapes the aurora takes, such as arcs, beads, and streamers) in movies from the CINEMA satellites |
| **How often** | A few campaigns a year | Continuously, for years | Any time, at your own pace |
| **What it gives the science** | Ground imagery with position, time, and viewing direction | Magnetometer, Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS), very low frequency (VLF), and high frequency (HF) radio data at known locations | A large labeled set of auroral movies |

All three groups contribute to the same three science questions, described on the [Science]({{ "/science/" | relative_url }}) page.

## Who runs MOVIE

MOVIE is part of the CINEMA mission team. Vincent Ledvina (University of Alaska Fairbanks) coordinates the aurora chaser campaigns. Kristina Collins and Nathaniel Frissell of HamSCI (Ham Radio Science Citizen Investigation) are the contacts for amateur radio operators, and the CINEMA team building the labeling app is the contact for aurora labelers.

An advisory board of aurora chasers and amateur radio operators reviews MOVIE's plans on behalf of volunteers.

## Meeting minutes

Summaries of MOVIE advisory board meetings are posted here, newest first.

### 2026-11-11: First advisory board meeting

13 of the 16 advisory board members joined Vincent Ledvina and Michael Hartinger on Zoom for the board's first meeting. The board members included aurora chasers from the United States, Canada, and Svalbard and amateur radio operators from HamSCI. The meeting lasted about an hour. Each member introduced themselves and their community, Vincent Ledvina presented the project, and the board gave feedback on the plan. The slides are published in the [CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie). The recording went to the board by email and is not posted here.

**Presentation.** The slides covered CINEMA and its three instruments, MOVIE's three science questions, the three volunteer communities (aurora chasers, amateur radio operators, and aurora labelers), planned and short-notice campaigns, the MOVIE timeline, and the volunteer interaction plan. They also covered this site, the announcement email list and forum, how aurora chasers will submit photographs through Zenodo, the labeling app, how the board will coordinate, and what MOVIE asks of the board. CINEMA is planned to launch in 2030, and MOVIE will continue past the current planning phase only if NASA approves it after the Preliminary Design Review (PDR) in January 2027.

**Feedback from the board.**

- **Submitting photographs.** Board members who created the accounts needed for Zenodo found the instructions clear. Uploading photographs and their details takes effort, so the board suggested that each community identify a few committed volunteers to do most of the submitting. A video walk-through of the steps, which Vincent Ledvina plans to record in a later phase, and a checklist for volunteers who help others submit would both be useful.
- **Location privacy.** An exact location is optional. Observers can give a general location, such as a town, in the public Zenodo record and may email an exact location privately to Vincent Ledvina. Exact positions could support analyses that combine several viewpoints of the same auroral structure, such as three-dimensional reconstructions, and they will not be used for anything else.
- **Quick links and materials.** The board asked for one page of quick links that sends volunteers to the right place for each task, and for half-page or postcard-sized graphics that explain CINEMA and MOVIE and why aurora chasers take part. This site is meant to serve as the quick-links page.
- **What to photograph.** Aurora near the path of a CINEMA satellite would be the most useful, with the camera direction recorded so that the images fall within the field of view of AIM, CINEMA's auroral imager. Unusual features such as STEVE (Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement) and SAR (stable auroral red) arcs would be the highest priority. The details will be set in later phases.
- **Science questions.** The three MOVIE science questions are the ones the team will use to show NASA that MOVIE can succeed. Volunteers are welcome to pursue their own questions with the data, and the team would like to encourage that.
- **Growing the communities.** The labeling app could introduce students to amateur radio and aurora photography, and the two communities could work together more.
- **Amateur radio stations.** Personal Space Weather Stations will be deployed in later phases, including new very low frequency (VLF), high frequency (HF), and Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) receivers that are in development. Stations are planned in Canada and possibly Alaska, and lower-latitude stations would also help. Finding hosts takes time, so outreach could start well before 2030. The [Host an Instrument]({{ "/host/" | relative_url }}) page lists where stations are needed.
- **Exchange visits and travel.** The team is budgeting for volunteer travel support. One idea is for HamSCI members to attend the Michigan Aurora Chasers workshop and for aurora chasers to attend the HamSCI Workshop in Scranton. Board members also asked about a field trip to the CINEMA launch, and Michael Hartinger will raise it with Robyn Millan, CINEMA's principal investigator.
- **International Polar Year.** The International Polar Year of 2032 to 2033 will overlap with CINEMA's operations, so preparation for the two efforts could be combined.

## MOVIE online

- **This site**, for campaigns, observing guidance, resources, and results.
- **The [CINEMA-MOVIE community on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/cinema-movie)**, where campaign images will be submitted and published, each with the observer's name and a citable DOI (digital object identifier).
- **The MOVIE email list and discussion forum**, which will open later in the mission. The CINEMA-MOVIE GitHub site will link to them once they are open.
